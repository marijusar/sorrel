Most app makers build their outbound around installs. A store adds a tool in your category, and within days a handful of competitors are in that merchant's inbox, all making some version of the same pitch. It's a reasonable instinct — that store just showed intent — but by the time you reach out, you're one voice among several saying the same thing at the same moment.

There's a quieter signal that deserves just as much attention: the removal. When a store drops an app in your category, it's telling you something an install never can.

## They've already paid for something like this

A store that installed and paid for a competing app has already cleared the hardest hurdle in any sales process: the decision to spend money on this category at all. You're not explaining why a tool like yours is worth budget — they've already agreed to that, in practice, with a card on file. The only question left is which tool earns that spend now.

## The problem was painful enough to justify moving off a working setup

Nobody drops a paid app lightly. Ripping out a tool that's wired into a store's workflow means broken automations, retraining staff, and downtime while the gap gets filled. Merchants only accept that cost when the tool stopped earning its keep — a bad experience, a missed need, a price that no longer matched the value. Whatever the reason, the problem underneath was real enough to justify the disruption of leaving. Put those two facts together and you get a lead that's both willing to pay and actively short a solution — not someone being introduced to the category for the first time, but someone re-evaluating who deserves their business right now.

The catch is that the window doesn't stay open forever. Give it a few weeks and the merchant has either found a replacement or shelved the problem — either way, the opening closes.
